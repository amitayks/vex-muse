# Why each rule exists (failure classes)

- **One dialect (spec verbatim):** skills in per-author formats made method
  undiscoverable and unreviewable across packs; ecosystem tooling reads the
  agentskills.io format, a fork buys nothing.
- **Budgets:** a bound skill loads whole into every factory item's context —
  bloat is a per-item production cost, not a style issue.
- **Version in frontmatter:** lines pin skill versions; silent edits under a
  stable version made production behavior drift from review.
- **Intent-not-case-study:** case-study prose froze one incident's details
  into doctrine; the reusable rule was buried and reapplied wrong.
- **Acceptance check:** "done" declared without a check shipped unfinished
  work repeatedly; the check must travel with the method.
- **Standalone law:** method that only worked inside a line could not be
  exercised, reviewed, or reused in conversation — the explore lane and the
  production lane diverged.
- **LEARNINGS same-day:** corrections held privately until "good enough"
  were lost to the org; a silent fix is a lost asset.
- **Method-free stations:** methodology duplicated between station prompts
  and skills disagreed within weeks; the station copy won silently in
  production while the skill copy won in review.
- **No identifiers:** real emails/hosts/repos shipped in early packs and
  cost history purges; first-pass identifiers are drafts.
