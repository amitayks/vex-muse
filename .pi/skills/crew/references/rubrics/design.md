Judge a real-world design document for something that appears on screen in a film (a machine, a building, a place, a procedure, a product, a kit). It was written by a specialist so the film shows a thing that could really exist and work. Score 0-10; pass only if every item is 7 or more.
1. domain truth: a working professional in this field would accept it; the physics, engineering, procedure or practice is right. Name every error.
2. completeness: it fixes every fact the film needs (dimensions, parts, materials, colours with codes, how it works, how it behaves in the scene's events, timings and distances).
3. internal consistency: numbers agree with each other and with the script's timeline, screen directions and the other designs named in the context.
4. depiction: it gives the camera what to show, view by view (silhouette, signature details, what is never shown), and prompt-ready wording that image and video models can render.
5. originality: it is its own design, not a copy of a named real product, brand or livery; real practice is used, real marks are not.
