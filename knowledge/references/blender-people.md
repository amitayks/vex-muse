# Blender people and first-person cameras

Built for the TOWER 42 one-take (a drone shot down to a man's eyes, then the lobby and the lift to level 42) on the principal's Mac ([[local-muse-mac-helper]]). Render settings, draft checks and the clip spec are in [[blender-field-notes]]. The paint pass over the renders is in [[render-repaint]].

## Rigged humans for $0
- **The route passed review.**
  - **The parts:**
    - MPFB 2.0.18, the MakeHuman plugin, from extensions.blender.org.
    - The MakeHuman system assets zip from files.makehumancommunity.org (281 MB of skins, eyes, hair, clothes and shoes).
    - CMU motion-capture walks in BVH form.
    - A retarget written in code: align rest directions, bake world rotations, resample 120 to 24 fps, and pin feet to the ground per frame. No retarget add-on is needed.
  - **Result:** the man reads as a person, with 0 float and 0 sink frames. The skin still looks a little CG, so the paint pass carries the last step.
  - **Licences:**

    | Part | Licence |
    |---|---|
    | MPFB | GPL-3, for the tool only; the characters it makes are not covered |
    | MakeHuman assets | CC0 |
    | CMU mocap | free for commercial use; credit it with "The data used in this project was obtained from mocap.cs.cmu.edu. The database was created with funding from NSF EIA-0196217." |
    | Poly Haven HDRIs, textures and plants | CC0 |
- **Give the elbow its own IK pole angle.** Elbows that shared the knee pole angle bent forward and inward up to the chin, and the first-person camera ended up inside his arm. With a separate elbow pole, the nearest body part in the lens is 0.37 m.
- **Pivot on the heel and toe, not a pelvis drop.** On the first-person man, a pelvis drop moved the eye 51.7 mm off the camera path. Heel and toe pivots kept the eye on the lens (0.0 mm).
- **A belly bulge can be a retarget fault, not a cloth fault.** The MPFB rest Hips bone leans back 32°, and the CMU retarget tilted the pelvis about 36° forward, which pushed the blouse out at the waist. Fix: the pelvis back to rest +3°, the LowerBack bone compensating, plus a flat-stomach target and a waist-front clamp. The crouch fix was the same kind: no pelvis roll, the stance leg extended to 0.997 and the stride shortened (worst knee median 17.9° over 48 walkers).

## Crowds
- **Solve each person's ground contact per frame, on the floor of their own level.** On a 70-person crowd, the first full build failed the contact gate: 23,337 sinking and 1,364 floating body-frames of 66,126. A per-frame solve on the deformed shoe mesh fixed both to 0. Each person uses the floor under them: the plaza and lobby at 0 m, the cab floor at its live height, level 42 at 168 m. Seated people sit with the pelvis on the seat and the soles on the floor. Walkers over a 0.16 m road dip needed a sidewalk rule.
- **An open collar can read as a hand at the chin.** Two walkers in the draft seemed to hold a hand at the chin. A scan found no wrist within 0.44 m of any head over all 70 people, and an object-ID pass put the skin shape inside the walker's own torso outline: it was an open collar and neck under motion blur. Measure before adding a prop. The fix is cloth: close deep V-necks to one button below the collarbone, or add an inner top, so no skin shows below the collarbone line.
- **Parent a person in a moving lift to the cab.** A man who did not move with the cab smeared in the motion blur of a mirror still.
- **The MakeHuman clothes library has no plain shirts or trousers.** For an office crowd, keep the blouse with a knee skirt, the button shirt, the striped shirt, the light jacket and the suit. Drop the logo tees, crop tops with leggings, overalls, denim shorts, camo sneakers and boots. The crew-neck `male_casualsuit02` has a logo, orange trims and a "makehuman.org" mark in its texture; tint it from its AO map instead (folds, no print). Repaint models re-dress clothes that look out of place ([[render-repaint]]).
- **Check a crowd on a per-variant sheet before the stills.** The first office-wardrobe sheet (20 variants) showed five defects: tinted tops that all read light grey, white ankle socks under knee skirts, blouses that balloon at the waist, crouched CMU walks (knee gate: a mid-stance bend of at most 20°, median per walker; the worst had 31°), and black ground patches from coincident faces. The second sheet passed with saturated tints, bare legs under skirts and the faces separated (black-flat area under 0.3 %).
- **MPFB hair: trace strands over each person's own card shell.** MPFB hair is alpha cards. A few smooth short styles with a glossy shader and near-black albedo (L* 11–16) read as helmets under a dark exposure, and the repaint keeps them ([[render-repaint]]).
  - **A groom grown from the scalp failed.** It lost every style (crops became frizzy masses) and came out 25–59 % lighter: it took the wardrobe-table colour, while the shell renders that colour times the texture luminance times 2.4. Strands 0.15 mm thick, tapering to 0.04 mm, are about 1/10 px at 1.3 m, and Cycles 5.2 has no minimum pixel width for curves, so they fogged; stray strands made a ghost head at a passenger's shoulder.
  - **Tracing the shell worked** on all 33 grooms (20 crowd variants and him). A sheet of front, 3/4 and back views passed Muse's eye check: no helmets remain. Strands follow the strand flow painted in the shell texture (structure-tensor orientation, mapped to 3-D through each triangle's UV Jacobian). They seed where alpha > 0.5 and stop where alpha < 0.25, below the brows or off the shell. They use the shell's own material through the shell UVs, with a per-person gain calibrated against the old shell's rendered L*, at 0.45 mm and tapered at both ends. His short hair kept its style at −2.3 % L*, and a black bob went from a plastic helmet to real hair.
  - **Strands inherit the shell's faults.** Where a long-hair shell passes inside a jacket, the strands do too, and the jacket shows through the hair in the back view. A re-groom that keeps every strand ≥ 3 mm outside the clothes and the body removed the patches; a little green stays between the strand tips where the hair lies over the jacket.
  - **Gates:** no strand more than 6 cm from the head or body, and |ΔL*| ≤ max(10 % of the old L*, 3.0). Near-black shells get their L* from gloss, and on near-black textures a gain flattens (gain 1 → 6 moved L* only 14 → 17), so dark hair still fails. Muse's ruling: waive those fails, because the lost L* is the plastic gloss that left; black strands at L* 12–18 are correct, and the gain is picked by eye. People under 12 px keep their cards (alpha edges, a hair-like shader, dark hair at L* 18–28); on near-black cards a lift in the hair's own hue reaches that range (12–14 → 21).
  - **Cost:** at full density, strands render at 9.2–9.8 s/frame (Cycles 32 spp, 720p).
- **A loose blouse proxy crumples at the shoulder.** One MPFB blouse came out inflated and lumpy over the shoulder, and the repaint kept it. A shrinkwrap to the skin made it worse: it snapped vertices up to 267 mm into white flaps, or stretched edges up to 12.5×. A corrective smooth on the shoulder group cannot tear; the 4 cm of shoulder volume it leaves on raised arms read as normal cloth in a paint test. The fitted blazer was rejected, because his light-blue shirt is his identity. Hard gates: spike excess against the rest garment ≤ 12 mm, and no more flipped faces than armature-only.
- **Mask skin that pokes through.** Skin that pokes out of a shirt shows as holes (a male shirt on a female body poked at both shoulders). Mask any skin vertex that is outside the garment in any pose, if its whole 1-ring stays covered in every pose. An inner top for an open collar fills only the collar opening, at least 2 mm under the garment. A vertex test can pass while skin shows: on large flat skin faces the corners sit inside the shirt, but the face centres bulge 1–12 mm through it. Check poses every 12 frames; one collar poke showed only in some walk phases.
  - **After two failed patches, change the garment.** Six patches failed on one open collar. Among them: a push along the normal dented the neck, deleting the skin opened a hole in the V, a wrap only shrank the patch, and two fabric overlays rendered as flat grey shapes. A top that closes at the neck, plus an inward skin push, closed the collar with no skin on any row (Muse's ruling: no third patch).
  - The inward push costs about 20 s per person, and on him it raised the poking faces from 41 to 46, so it runs only where a fault shows.
- **Crowd variants repeat as clones.** In the stills, 3 of 5 people in one frame wore the same light-blue striped knit, which also hid the hero's light-blue shirt, his marker. The limits, which passed: the blue family on at most 4 of 20 variants, one top on at most 2 variants, no top like the hero's, and no shared variant or top colour within 6 m.

## Hands and held props
- **Solve the grip on a held prop.** Before a grip solve, the hand stayed 93 mm from the cup. After it, the fingers and thumb were within 1.5 mm and the palm within 1.8 mm, with no penetration.
- **Release in order.** On the cup-down beat the hand edge cut 12.5 mm into the desk, so the grip moved 26 mm higher on the cup. The fingers passed through the cup on release, so the fingers now open in place before the hand pulls out over the desk edge.
- **A card tap needs the card at the reader.** An empty hand on a card reader is a logic error the eye catches, and he cannot lean in, because his eye is the lens. A badge reel pulled from the lanyard passed every gate but failed the eye check: the card trailed under his forearm as a detached white shape. The simple route replaced it: the card is already in his hand before the tap, held over the reader, and back on the reel out of view. A white card face up read as a blank tile, so it faces the reader.
- **Blend out a reach after the release.** The clavicle (3-bone) reach stayed on after the cup release, and the upper arm swung through the lower-left corner at 0.228 m from the lens. Blend it out over 0.25 s from the release, and gate own-body clearance inside the release search.

## First-person camera
- **First-person gate**, measured per frame from the eye (`eyes.json`). These are the studio's rules, and the TOWER 42 scene passes them:
  - Surfaces stay ≥ 0.6 m from the eye, except during a hand contact.
  - Doorway jambs may come to ≥ 0.35 m for ≤ 1.0 s.
  - His own body stays ≥ 0.25 m. Hide his head and neck from the camera only, and keep them for the mirror. Start to hide them before the lens reaches them: a late start gave two dark frames as the lens passed through the back of his head, and a start at 0.30 m cleared the blank scan.
  - **A patch that replaces his hair must keep the lens trick.** A strand patch set his new hair camera-invisible (mirror only) and wired the strand shader past the lens mix, so he was bald to the film camera until the lens entered his head (about f451), while the drone shot sees his crown from above. The same patch sized each groom by on-screen head height, and that scan skipped him, so he got only 4,000 strands. Carry the lens-hide keys and the camera visibility from the old part to the new one, and check the head through the film camera. Restoring both, with 30,318 strands, cut the scalp seen from above from 11.9 % to 0.01 %, and in the merge from 12.2 % to 0.15 % (gate ≤ 0.5 %).
  - **An eye-locked pelvis can lift his feet.** While his head pitched down for the glance, the pelvis solve kept the eye on the lens and raised the hips past the legs' reach, so his feet left the cab floor by up to 21.6 mm for about 20 frames. The contact gate called them seen, because it counts anyone within 3 m of the lens through a mirror proxy. A ray test from the lens and through the mirror found 0 px of foot, so the lift was accepted. Ray-test "seen" before you fix a contact fail.
  - A cup at a sip may come to ≥ 0.08 m for ≤ 1.2 s, in the lower half of the frame.
  - Other people stay ≥ 0.8 m, except a pass-by of ≤ 0.7 s.
  - A blank-frame scan allows no run over 1.5 s.
  - Waits get things to look at, and head turns are quick (0.3–0.5 s) with holds.
- **A hand beat must happen in frame.** With the gaze locked level, every desk spot within his reach (about 0.70 m from the shoulder) was below the frame, so his cup-down beat was out of view, and the repaint model invented one. The decision: unlock the gaze for that beat only, as a pitch-only dip of at most 25° with a smooth ease and a peak rate of at most 60°/s, because a person looks at what he puts down. The built dip peaks at 41.5°/s, and the cup lands in the lower left of the frame.
- **A glance at a held prop brings the prop to the eye.** A straight arm with the cup far out read as a thumbs-up, and a 60° dip showed only a passenger's shoulders. The ruling: the cup comes to the chest (elbow bent 80–100°, about 38° below the eye line), the thumb wraps the cup, and the gaze dips 26° to the lid. Built: the cup at 0.37 m and 4° left, because 0.36 m and 10° bent the elbow to 104°. Time a gaze move from the angle it really travels: from the old up-gaze at the display the turn is 42.8°, not 26°, so the dip takes 1.45 s at a 55.8°/s peak. With a 24 mm lens (±23° vertical) that drops the floor display out of frame for the 1 s hold, which is accepted, because a man who checks his coffee looks at the coffee. The lid must be fully seen in every hold frame, because the lid tells the repaint "coffee": every lid vertex inside |NDC| ≤ 0.95, and at least 97 % of the camera-facing lid samples are first hits. In the glance, fingers on the cup with the palm 19–25 mm off is how people hold a coffee, so the 2 mm grip gate does not apply there (Muse's ruling).
- **A mirror beat needs a real mirror and the right shirt.** A brushed-steel lift wall gave a dark, soft reflection, and his "light-blue" shirt read navy even in a glass mirror (reflected value 0.107), because his outfit texture was dark navy. The shirt faces get light blue (about sRGB 170/195/225) on their own, and the panel he looks at is silvered glass (about 8 % darker, a 3 mm bevel, a steel frame). The read gate on the hero frame: his face, lanyard, cup and lid reflected and not blocked, and the reflected shirt at hue 195–225°, saturation 0.15–0.60, value ≥ 0.45.
- **The end of the one-take needs two beats.** From his seated eye the cup sits 46° below the horizon, and a 28 mm frame is 39.8° tall, so the cup and the city cannot share one frame; the cup was also 61° right of the window view, so the yaw unlocks too. The end is a desk beat (cup, keyboard, monitor) held 0.75 s, then a min-jerk drift at no more than 25°/s to the city through his window, then a 2 s hold with breathing only. A 0.35 s desk beat was too short for the eye to find the cup. When a read and the length conflict, the film gets longer, not the read shorter: TOWER 42 grew from 2,448 to 2,520 frames.
  - **Check the last frame for blockers.** A tall white desk screen filled about 40 % of the final hold and hid the city. Lowered to 0.30 m, it still left the city at 30 % of the frame. Muse's ruling: remove the screen between his window-side desk and the window, and raise the end pitch from −12° to about −5°, for a city of at least 50 % with the desk edge at the bottom. Built, the drift is 39.5° and peaks at 22.1°/s on the same frames. The end frame passed Muse's eye check: the city fills the window, and the desk corner and monitor edge hold the lower right.
