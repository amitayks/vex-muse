# K-pop music-video direction — how attention is managed

Why it matters: K-pop MVs are engineered for re-watch and clip-sharing; they
are the best public playbook for holding attention for 3 minutes.

**Rules behind it**
- **Cut on the grid, vary the grid.** Verses cut every 1–2 bars; pre-chorus
  tightens to every beat; the chorus drop gets a hard reset (new set, new
  outfit, wide formation). Density tracks song energy — map cuts to
  `song.json` energy, not to taste.
- **The center.** Every frame has one clear center (the member singing, the
  hero object). Formations are arrows pointing at the center. In our work
  the protagonist or the hero lyric is the center; everything else is a
  pointer.
- **Point choreography / "killing part".** One signature, instantly
  imitable gesture tied to the hook lyric, repeated every chorus, shot the
  same way each time (frontal, medium-wide) so it becomes a meme. Design it
  first; it is the most shareable 3 seconds.
- **Color blocking per world.** Each set has one dominant color and one
  accent; sets rotate with sections. The palette change *is* the section
  change signal.
- **Solo insert cadence.** Group/wide → solo close-up → object insert →
  group, roughly every 2 bars. Close-ups land on emotionally loaded words.
- **Camera on the beat.** Whip pans, snap zooms and speed-ramps land on
  downbeats; slow push-ins under held notes. Never a dead static frame.
- **Fandom Easter eggs.** Tiny symbols (numbers, props, lyric fragments on
  signs) placed for the second viewing — rewards re-watching and threads.
- **Bookended narrative.** Cold open with a mystery image, callback at the
  end. The MV is a loop.

**Our translation**: backup dancers = a troupe of the protagonist's
variants; formations on phrase boundaries; one killing-part gesture on the
hook; per-chorus set/palette escalation; object inserts carry the zeitgeist
references between dance beats.
