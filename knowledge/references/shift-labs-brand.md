# Shift Labs: brand evidence for every Shift Labs document

Shift Labs (the company behind Vex) has three brand sources, and they disagree on purpose: a **pink keycap mark** with a lowercase "shift" wordmark, a **Swiss editorial research look** (warm paper, bold neo-grotesque titles, one signal orange) from its Agent Work Index decks, and the **Vex dashboard theme** (charcoal dark mode, white light mode, plum accent, gemstone status colours). Skill `shift-labs-design` (`.pi/skills/shift-labs-design/`) turns them into one kit; this note keeps the measured evidence and the reasons behind its choices.

## The wordmark and lockup
- The lockup is the keycap plus lowercase **"shift"**, navy on light and white on the dark variant. In the 640×264 retina lockup the keycap spans (39,38)–(217,217) and the wordmark (258,86)–(533,190), aspect 2.64.
- **The wordmark is Inter Bold** (wght 700, opsz 14) at about **−0.025 em** tracking. Matching by pixel IoU against the lockup: Inter 700/opsz 14 scored 0.885, ahead of weight 600 and opsz 32; the rebuilt vector lockup overlays at IoU 0.87. The official lockup's arrow is slightly wider than the avatar arrow (the favicon's proportions).
- **Logo rule, current (the principal, 2026-09-29 20:41): "keep only the logo without its white background… we do not use any white background."** It replaces 03:31's "dont cut the original logo. it is as it is." Documents use the official artwork with only the white 512×512 `<rect>` removed. The frame is set to the logo's own border (the lip's outer edge), computed from the path geometry: viewBox `162.89 130.73 232 232.75`. Paths, colours and proportions stay byte-identical, and all 16 colourways get the same trim. Never put it on a white square, chip or tile, and never recolour, redraw, re-crop it or build a lockup. The skill keeps the untouched sources in `assets/brand/official/`; `scripts/trim_logo.py` derives `logo.svg`, `logo.png` (transparent) and `avatars/`, and `PINS.json` pins every hash.
- **Visual analysis of the official file.** The logo fills 45.4% of the square's width and sits off-centre: white margins 162.9 left, 117.1 right, 130.7 top, 148.5 bottom. Its border is the lip `#CB8E97`: 22 units along the bottom (the keycap's side), 2 on the right, a hairline on the left, rising at the top-right corner. By area the logo is face 79.8%, lip 10.8%, arrow 9.0%.

**Shape vocabulary:** tick strips (one tick per run, orange = fired), outlined source lists (Inbox, Bookings, Sheet…), dark code cards, arrows (grey = normal path, orange = firing), event boxes, agent nodes, outcome chips, stacked squares for the cursor store, skeleton bars for mock UIs, reminder timelines with growing ringed dots, before/after state cards ("gate B4 ≠ gate B9"), mismatch tables with dashed connectors, deadline windows, range bands on a line, a 24-hour axis, probe rows of ✓/✗, codebook share bars (top bar orange, the rest grey), a stage staircase (Shadow → Notify first → Propose only → Live → Autonomous).

**Voice and content model.** Plain, present-tense declaratives, the same simplified English as the Vex tours ([[vex-interactive-explainers]]). Each data slide runs eyebrow → title → one-sentence lede → panel (diagram + EXAMPLES) → an orange stat → a "How we counted." methodology footnote. **What it says** (useful for any later Shift Labs or Vex piece): 8 fire patterns: new items 53%, a set time 18%, a state changed 8%, something is down 8%, no reply in time 5%, a number out of range 5%, two systems disagree 3%, a deadline is near 1%. 58% of sensors run every 5 min or more often, 27% every minute. 66% of sensor-started turns end with nothing to do, and 49% of those fall outside office hours or at the weekend. About 80% of sensors wake the agent when they fire and 17% do the work in code. 31% of sensors had their code rewritten at least once. Recurring techniques: set a baseline first, give each event an id, wait for a second failure, hold alerts overnight.

## Decisions shipped in `shift-labs-design` (1.0.0; logo rules 1.1.0 → 1.2.0, 2026-09-29; decision-ready 1.4.0, 2026-09-30)
The skill is the authority; its `references/brand-dna.md` holds the token table. Project history: `shift-labs-design`. The choices and their reasons:
- **Light mode is the deck's warm paper** (the publication identity); **dark mode is the dashboard's charcoal ramp** (`#181818` / `#212121` / `#2b2b2b`, with `#101010` as the darkest well for code). There are two themes, not three. The default is light, like the dashboard, with a persisted toggle and a `?theme=dark` override for screenshots.
- **One accent, three text tiers:** signal orange means "look here / it fired", and grey carries everything else. The dashboard's gem palette is not imported. Fills and marks keep `#ff5a1f`. Text uses `--s-signal-label` `#e6511c` (3.6:1: eyebrows, bold caps labels, stat numbers) or `--s-signal-ink` `#b33c10` (≥ 4.5:1 on paper *and* on the peach tint; `#bf4417` failed on the tint at 4.19). Dark: label `#ff6a33`, ink `#ff8a5c`.
- **Muted deepened** from the deck's `#6b6f78` to `#63666f`. The deck grey is 4.27:1 on the stone well; the new one is 4.86:1 and looks the same.
- **Caps-label rule:** shoot fails any text under 3:1 and warns on small reading text under 4.5:1. Bold uppercase labels are exempt from the warning at ≥ 3:1, a deliberate deviation matching the decks.
- **Inter everywhere, embedded:** Inter is the brand's own measured wordmark face, is open (OFL) and renders the same on every host. Helvetica Neue is not on the render box, and PP Formula / Cinetype carry the licence doubt above. Inter Text 400/700 is for body, Inter Display 700 (opsz 32) for titles, which reads closer to Helvetica Neue's tight display cut. **DejaVu Sans Mono** is used for code: it is Menlo's open ancestor and matches the deck's code cards.
- **Fonts** are subset to woff2 (about 25 KB per cut) covering Latin, punctuation, arrows, math, ₪ €, ✓ ✗ and geometric shapes, with a `coverage.json` for the lint. Inter has ✓ ✗ × → ≈ ≠ ▲ ▼ ₪ ⇧ ↵ but **lacks ✕ ◉ ⏰ Δ**. DejaVu Sans Mono has ✕ and ◉. Neither font has Δ, so tables write "Change" instead. Draw anything else outside coverage as a shape ([[generation-field-notes]]).
- **Logo placement (1.2.0):** trimmed logo as an `<img>`, sized by height only: page header 28 px, footer 22 px, deck cover 40 pt, closing slide 64 pt. Minimum 16 px, with a quarter of its width clear on every side. There is no background behind it in either mode.
- **Deck output** is authored in pt at 960×540 (= 1280×720 CSS px; every size is `N * var(--u)` with `--u: 1pt` in a deck). A named `@page slide { size: 960pt 540pt }` prints one reference-size PDF page per slide, while pages print A4. Naming: CSS prefix `s-`, variables `--s-*`, JS global `SL`, status `window.__shift`.
- **Fifteen shapes** drawn from JSON. Ten explain a system: ticks, axis, line, shares, flow, queue, diff, match, stairs, fanout. Five carry decision data (1.4.0): columns, variance, tablegraph, bullet, heatmap. `line` also takes several series. All are coloured only through `k-*` classes, so a theme toggle redraws nothing by hand.
- **Data colour keeps the one-orange rule (1.4.0).** On a chart, orange marks the key bar, the series the text discusses, or what missed its target. Everything else is grey context, and the comparison period is an outlined bar beside the current one. Axis ticks are round steps (1, 2, 2.5 or 5 × 10ⁿ).
- **Evidence levels are ink pips, never orange.** A `.s-claim` chip shows four pips for confirmed, down to none for unknown, in ink and grey. Orange means "it fired", and how strong the evidence is doesn't count as an event. The method behind the chips: the decision-ready method in skill `shift-labs-design`.

## How it was measured (reusable)
- **A PDF's design, exactly:** PyMuPDF `page.get_text('dict')` spans give (font, size, colour) per role. `page.get_drawings()` gives fills weighted by area, strokes, widths, dashes, and corner radii read from the curve control points of rounded rects. Read the text first, then a low-res contact sheet of the pages is justified for a visual reference like this one.
- **Tooling off the shared volume:** the box has no PDF or font libraries. `uv venv /tmp/pdfenv` plus `uv pip install pymupdf fonttools pillow numpy brotli` works, and `/tmp` is a separate overlay with ~20 GB free, while `/data` had 2.9 GB ([[tool-selection]]).
- **Identifying a wordmark font:** instance the variable font per (wght, opsz) with `fontTools.varLib.instancer`, render the word at several trackings, and score IoU against the lockup's ink mask. Then rebuild the word as SVG paths (`SVGPathPen` + `TransformPen`) and overlay it through headless Chrome. Instancing 8 cuts of Inter took minutes on the 2-CPU box, so run it as a background execution.
- **Instanced cuts keep the variable font's names.** `instantiateVariableFont` leaves the `name` table as it was, so every static cut still reports itself as "Inter Regular". Chrome still draws the bold on screen, but the printed PDF's font list showed only `Inter-Regular`. Before saving each woff2, rewrite name IDs 1, 2, 4, 6, 16 and 17 (family, subfamily, full name, PostScript name, typographic family and subfamily) with `name.setName(value, id, 3, 1, 0x409)`. After that the PDF lists `Inter-Bold` and `InterDisplay-Bold` as separate fonts.
